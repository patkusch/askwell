# Change impact assessment

Two kinds: impact of a **change request** on a project, and impact of the **project's change** on the organisation.

## A. A change request on the project

1. Describe the change and who asked.
2. Trace it through requirements, design, tests and plan (`requirements-traceability` shows what it touches).
3. Estimate effect on **cost, date, quality, risk, other work**.
4. List options: accept now, defer to a later phase, accept in exchange for dropping something else, reject.
5. Recommend one, and send it to the person with authority (`scope-definition`). Log the decision (`decision-log`).

## B. The project's effect on the organisation

For each affected group (team, role, customer, supplier), ask:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Work | What do they do differently from day one? |
| Skills | What new skills? How long to learn? |
| Tools | What systems change or go away? |
| Data | What do they enter or see differently? |
| Roles | Do jobs, reporting lines or workload change? |
| Rules | New policies, controls, approvals? |
| Customers | What do customers notice? |

Rate the effect: **high** (job changes), **medium** (steps change), **low** (aware only). Then plan:

- **Communication**: who hears what, from whom, when.
- **Training**: format, length, timing close to go-live.
- **Support**: floor-walkers, a hotline, a quick-reference sheet for the first weeks.
- **Readiness check**: ask representatives before go-live; delay if not ready.
- **Resistance**: what do they lose? Address it directly.

## Output

Table by group: impact, rating, actions, owner, date. Highlight the three groups with the highest impact and the lowest readiness.
