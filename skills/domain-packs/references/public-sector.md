# Public sector

**Questions to add**
- What law or policy gives the organisation the duty? Requirements must trace to it.
- Who is the service for, including people who cannot or will not use a digital route? What is the alternative?
- How is success measured for the public (time taken, completion rate, cost per case), not only for the organisation?
- Is it bought through a procurement route with fixed rules and timelines?
- Which records are public, which are protected, and who answers information requests?

**Typical requirements to propose**
- A route for people without digital access, and for people who need help.
- Accessibility to the standard the body must meet, tested with disabled users.
- Plain language at a stated reading level.
- Clear eligibility rules, with the source text for each rule and a way to explain a decision to the person.
- A way to appeal or ask for review of a decision.
- Audit trail and records management, with retention schedules.
- Equality impact considered and recorded.
- Value-for-money evidence and a benefits plan with owners.
- Security classification of data and where it may be stored.
- Open standards and exit options, so the body is not locked in.

**Traps**
- Treating the digital service as the whole service. The phone line and the paper form still exist.
- Rules written in policy language that nobody can turn into a test; ask the policy owner for worked examples.
- Fixed procurement timelines that leave no time for discovery.
- Success counted as "went live" instead of "people got what they needed".
- Automated decisions with no human review or explanation.

**Rules to confirm** (names only): equality law, accessibility regulations for public bodies, freedom-of-information law, data protection, procurement rules, records-management rules, and any rules on automated decision-making.
